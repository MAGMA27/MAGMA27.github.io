Let me start with the conclusion: I believe insight and expression will be the most important skills in the age of AI.

If you have read the original Transformer paper, you know that it was designed for text translation. Translation is what AI excels at; at its core, it is a node that can (with a high probability) process information correctly.

From the perspective of translation, writing code means translating logic and algorithms into a language that machines can understand. Writing contracts, legal documents, papers, and so on also means translating ideas into languages with specific standards. Here is a provocative claim: information that can be encoded can be processed correctly by AI. In the AIGC field, scripts and storyboards are translated into images. In the past, humans performed these "translation" tasks; now AI can do them. So careers that make you a node in an information flow are at risk. If we extend the concept of "translation" further, is industrial root-cause analysis the process of translating a product's failure symptoms into an inference chain based on physical laws, and then translating that chain into the language of design to close the loop? Fields once considered high-barrier and heavily dependent on experience can be handled by AI as long as their domain knowledge can be put on paper.

Why do I believe insight and expression will not be replaced?

Insight is long-term judgment: judging the relationships between things. It is the starting point and entry point for using AI. For AI to find that entry point on its own, it would need to establish relationships across extremely broad fields and very long time spans, then navigate a high-dimensional state space to find valuable and meaningful paths. That is the core of human value, and AI still cannot do it well. So in the future, HOW will become less important, while WHAT and WHY will become more important. In academia, this means choosing a genuinely meaningful research question. In industry, it means finding a process that is genuinely worth optimizing or reducing costs in.

Expression matters because humans still cannot encode thought, and machines have not learned mind-reading. We have to rely on expression to turn our ideas into reality. Behind AI-generated comics, the director becomes the only indispensable person because the production process is about moving the ideas in their head into the outside world. Expression is the bridge through which "intent -> specification -> implementation" flows. It gradually makes an abstract intention concrete; only by controlling this process can we get AI to accurately implement what is in our minds. Good expression requires a sufficiently deep understanding of terminology. Terms such as "24mm wide-angle lens", "first principles", and "factory pattern" can immediately align the AI with the concepts in your head. So even in the AI era, basic learning, practice, and experience remain important—perhaps even more important than before. Whether you understand things from first principles almost determines the ceiling of your expression. Someone else's AI may get something right in one pass, while a person who cannot express themselves well may iterate ten times and still produce something mediocre. That is where the gap opens up.
